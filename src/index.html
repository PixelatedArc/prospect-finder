    How the whole process works
    1. Find. Run a niche and city. Weak sites are scored and given a Priority (High, Medium, Low) from their Google reviews, how weak the site is, whether a personal email and owner name were found, and whether an agency already looks after the site.
    2. Email. Review the first emails, best prospects first, and create drafts or send. The Status column in the sheet changes to drafted or sent.
    3. Check replies. Open the app (or press "Check Gmail for replies"). Anyone who answered is marked replied, and a reply containing their audit summary and your offer is waiting in your Gmail Drafts. Read it, adjust it, send it.
    4. Follow up once. After 4 days of silence the business appears under "Follow-ups due". One short follow-up, then it is left alone.
    5. Record the outcome. In the sheet, set the Status dropdown to call booked, won or lost. The app never overwrites those.
    6. Read the scorecard. The Scorecard tab shows reply rate, calls and wins for each niche and city. Keep the searches that get replies and drop the ones that do not.

    "No thanks" replies and bounced addresses go to the Do Not Contact tab automatically.

    Sending limits and how to stay out of spam

    Limits built into this app

    • Only sites marked WEAK that publish an email address get an email, and only after you press a button.
    • At most your daily number (never above 40) per day, counted in your sheet so it holds across all your PCs.
    • A pause of 1.5 to 4 minutes between two emails, so they do not go out as a burst.
    • Nobody is emailed twice: every draft and send is recorded in the Email Log tab.
    • Anyone listed in the Do Not Contact tab (an address or a whole domain) is always skipped. When someone replies "no thanks", add them there the same day.
    • Plain text only, no tracking pixels, no attachments.

    Google's limits

    • A free @gmail.com account can send to about 500 recipients a day, but only about 100 a day through a connector like this one. Google Workspace allows about 2,000 and 1,500. Going over locks sending for up to 24 hours.
    • Gmail expects the share of your emails that recipients mark as spam to stay under 0.3%, and ideally under 0.1%. With 10 emails a day, a single spam report is already far over that, so relevance matters more than volume.
    • Mail from your own domain must pass SPF and DKIM checks or Gmail may reject it. Anyone sending 5,000+ a day also needs DMARC and one-click unsubscribe; you are far below that.

    What keeps you in the inbox

    • Start slow. Week 1: 5 to 10 a day. Week 2: 15 to 20. Stay at or under 30 to 40 per mailbox after that. A new or rarely used mailbox that suddenly sends 50 cold emails gets flagged.
    • Use a separate domain for outreach (for example a variation of your business name on Google Workspace), with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up, and use it normally for 2 to 3 weeks first. A free @gmail.com works for very small volume but looks less trustworthy and puts your personal account at risk.
    • Send on weekdays in business hours, and reply quickly to anyone who answers. Replies are the strongest signal that your mail is wanted.
    • Keep the first email short, specific and link-free. The findings line about their own site is what makes it not look like bulk mail. Edit each one a little if you can.
    • Prefer a personal address over info@ or office@. Generic mailboxes are read by staff and are more likely to report spam.
    • One follow-up at most, 4 to 5 days later, sent by hand as a reply to your first email. Then stop.
    • Watch for bounces. If more than 1 in 20 addresses bounce, pause and check the addresses before sending more.

    The law (US, CAN-SPAM)

    • Say honestly who you are and what the email is about. No misleading subject lines.
    • Include a real postal address (a registered office or PO box is fine).
    • Give a way to opt out and honor it within 10 business days. Here that means adding them to Do Not Contact.
    • These rules are for US businesses. For the other countries in the app:
    • UK and Ireland: cold email to companies (Ltd, LLP, PLC) is allowed with your identity and an opt-out. Sole traders and partnerships need consent first.
    • Canada (CASL): consent is normally needed first. The common exception is an address the business publishes on its own site, a message about their business, and no "no unsolicited email" notice on the site.
    • Australia and New Zealand: similar to Canada: a published business address plus a message relevant to their business, unless the site says otherwise.
    • This is general guidance, not legal advice. Outside the US, create drafts and check each site before sending.
